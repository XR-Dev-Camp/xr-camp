# Challenge 2: Creative

**Optional.** Roughly 45–60 minutes.

Write your own STRIDE-style threat model for a small app from your own life, culture, or community — not this lesson's app.

## Task

1. Pick a small, real (or realistic) app you know well: a community group's sign-up form, a local market's order chat, a school's homework portal, a neighbourhood mutual-aid spreadsheet turned into a web form — anything with accounts, or user-submitted content, or someone's personal data.
2. Write a STRIDE table for it, in the same shape as this lesson's README: one row per **S**poofing, **T**ampering, **R**epudiation, **I**nformation disclosure, **D**enial of service, and **E**levation of privilege threat, each naming a *concrete* thing that could go wrong in *that specific app* — not the generic category description.
3. For at least three rows, name a specific, realistic fix — referencing an OWASP Cheat Sheet, an MDN article, or another primary source where one applies, the same way this lesson's own README does.
4. Note which threat, for your app, would be the most damaging if left unfixed, and say why — who is harmed, and how.

## Why this matters

STRIDE is a way of thinking, not a fact about any one codebase — the skill this lesson wants you to keep is asking "what could go wrong here, specifically" about *any* app, including ones you did not build and are not being graded on. Doing it once for something you care about, in your own words, is what makes the habit stick.

## Done when

- [ ] A STRIDE table for an app of your choosing, with a concrete (not generic) threat in every row.
- [ ] At least three rows name a specific, sourced fix.
- [ ] A short note on which threat matters most for this app, and why.
