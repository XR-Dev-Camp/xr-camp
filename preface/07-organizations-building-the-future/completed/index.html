Who builds the web

The organisations that write the web's rules, and the technologies each one looks after. By Ana.

The map

Show the organisations behind:

  • W3C

    The World Wide Web Consortium, founded by Tim Berners-Lee in 1994. Writes most of the web's standards.

    • CSS
    • WCAG
    • SVG
    • WebXR
    • WebGPU

    Anyone can join a W3C Community Group for free.

  • WHATWG

    A group formed by browser makers in 2004. Maintains the HTML Living Standard, which W3C also endorses.

    • HTML
    • DOM
    • Fetch
    • URL

    Anyone can comment on its standards on GitHub.

  • Ecma International

    Its committee TC39 maintains ECMAScript, the standard behind JavaScript, with a new edition every year.

    • JavaScript (ECMAScript)

    TC39's proposals are discussed openly on GitHub.

  • IETF

    The Internet Engineering Task Force. Writes the internet's protocols as documents called RFCs.

    • HTTP
    • TLS
    • DNS

    Anyone can take part through its mailing lists, with no membership.

  • Khronos Group

    A consortium of companies that write graphics and 3D standards.

    • WebGL
    • glTF
    • OpenXR
    • KTX

    Membership is paid, but its specifications and samples are free to use.

  • Web3D Consortium

    Looks after X3D, the ISO standard for 3D scenes that grew from VRML.

    • X3D
    • VRML
    • H-Anim

    Its specifications are free to read online.

  • Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC)

    Writes standards for maps and location data, so that maps from different sources can work together.

    • Web map services
    • CityGML
    • 3D Tiles

    Its standards are free to download.

  • Metaverse Standards Forum

    Founded in 2022. Not a standards body itself: it brings standards organisations and companies together so their standards fit.

    • Coordination between standards

    Joining is free for organisations.

  • Open Source Initiative (OSI)

    Maintains the Open Source Definition and approves which licences count as open source.

    • Open source licences

    Anyone can take part in its public discussions.

  • XR Guild

    A professional association for people who work in XR, focused on ethics and responsible practice.

    • Ethical principles for XR

    Individuals can become members.

How a standard is made

  1. An idea. Someone has a problem the web cannot solve yet.
  2. A community group or explainer. People write down the problem and possible solutions, in public.
  3. A working group. Browser makers, companies, and experts agree to write a specification.
  4. Drafts and review. The draft is reviewed for accessibility, privacy, security, and internationalisation.
  5. Implementations and tests. Browsers build it, and shared tests check they all behave the same.
  6. A standard. Once it works in more than one browser, it becomes a recommendation.
