My ethics audit and pledge

What I found when I audited a website, and the promises I make for everything I build. By Ana.

What I audited

MegaDeals, a fictional shop made for this lesson. It looks like many real shops. I tested it with my keyboard, read every word, including the small print, and filled in the form without submitting it.

Accessibility

Accessibility problems I found
ProblemWho it harmsHow to fix it
The product image has no alt textBlind people using a screen reader hear nothing, or the file nameAdd alt="Black wireless headphones"
"Buy now" is a div, not a buttonKeyboard users cannot reach or press itUse <button>
Form fields have no labelsScreen-reader users do not know what to typeUse <label> for every field
The small print is very light greyPeople with low vision, or anyone on a phone in sunlightDark enough text to meet WCAG contrast

Privacy and data

Privacy problems I found
ProblemWhy it is wrongHow to fix it
It asks for date of birth, phone, and national ID numberA shop needs none of these to sell headphones. Data you never collect can never leak.Ask only for what the order needs
"Share my information with our partners" is already tickedConsent must be a real choice, made by the personLeave it unticked, or remove it
Cancelling needs a phone call in a 15-minute windowLeaving should be as easy as joiningAdd a cancel button in the account

Deceptive design

Deceptive patterns I found
PatternWhat it does
Fake urgencyThe countdown starts again every time the page loads. The offer never really ends.
Hidden costA $12.50 fee appears only in tiny grey text, and is added at checkout.
ConfirmshamingThe "No thanks, I don't like saving money" link makes you feel foolish for saying no.
Pre-ticked boxesYou agree to daily emails and data sharing unless you notice and untick them.
Hard to cancelEasy to join, almost impossible to leave.

AI and honesty

"Sofia from the MegaDeals team" is presented as a person, but a real shop would almost certainly use an automated chatbot here. People have a right to know when they are talking to a machine. And "picked just for you" gives no reason for the choice: I do not know what data it used, or whether it shows different prices to different people.

Fix: label the chat as an automated assistant, and explain in plain words why a product is recommended.

My pledge

As a developer and designer, I promise to:

  1. Build for everyone: keyboard, screen reader, slow connection, old phone.
  2. Collect only the data I need, and ask clearly before I collect it.
  3. Never use fake urgency, hidden costs, or guilt to change someone's choice.
  4. Say when people are talking to AI, and check AI output before I trust it.
  5. Show people and cultures, including my own, with respect.
  6. Keep people safe and comfortable in 3D and XR: no forced movement, and a way out.
  7. Credit the work of others, and use only what I have the right to use.
