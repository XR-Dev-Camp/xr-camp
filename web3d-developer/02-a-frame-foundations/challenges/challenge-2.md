# Challenge 2: Creative

**Optional.** Roughly 60 minutes.

A texture pattern from your own culture, and a welcome panel in your own language.

## Task

1. Design a simple, repeating pattern that means something where you are from (a textile motif, a tile pattern, a border design), as an SVG, the same way `assets/woven-pattern.svg` was made: simple shapes, one or two colours, small enough to stay well under the 1 MB texture budget.
2. Turn it into a PNG the same way this lesson did (a screenshot of the SVG rendered in a browser, or any tool you prefer), and use it on the floor or the pedestal in place of the woven pattern.
3. Add a welcome panel in a language that matters to you. First, test whether A-Frame's default font can show it: write the word into a plain `<a-text>` in a scratch page, and look at the result carefully, the way this lesson tested Spanish and Chinese. If any letter is missing or wrong, use a canvas-drawn texture instead, as the Chinese panel does.
4. Record your texture's source in `ATTRIBUTION.md`, the same way `woven-pattern.png` is recorded, even if you made it yourself.

## Why this matters

The room's structure (primitives, textures, `<a-assets>`, and canvas text) transfers to any culture's visual language and any script. Testing a new language against the default font, rather than assuming, is the habit this lesson most wants you to keep.

## Done when

- [ ] A new, self-made texture pattern replaces or joins the woven one, credited in `ATTRIBUTION.md`.
- [ ] A welcome panel exists in a language of your choice, tested against the default font first.
- [ ] If the default font could not show it, it is drawn as a canvas texture instead, and renders correctly.
