# Challenge 1: Foundation

**Required.** Roughly 45 minutes.

Add a licence and credits to your repository.

## Task

1. **Choose a licence for your code.** If you are not sure, use the MIT Licence, as in [`completed/LICENSE-example`](../completed/LICENSE-example).
2. **Add it in the browser:** on your repository's front page, choose **Add file → Create new file**, and name the file `LICENSE`. Choose **Choose a license template**, pick the licence, check that the year and your name are right, and commit with the message `Add MIT licence for the code`.
3. **Credit everything you did not make.** Create `ATTRIBUTION.md` (or fill in the **Credits** section of your README) with a table like this one:

   ```markdown
   | What | Made by | Where from | Licence |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | A-Frame 1.8.0 | The A-Frame authors | https://aframe.io/ | MIT |
   | Lessons and starter files | XR Camp | XR Camp projects repository | See its LICENSE-CODE and LICENSE-CONTENT |
   ```

   Add a row for every photo, icon, font, or library you used. If you took the photo yourself, say so.
4. **Say what covers your words and pictures.** In your README's **Licence** section, write which licence covers the code, and what covers everything else: for example, "Words and pictures: © 2026 your name, all rights reserved".
5. **Do TODO 10** in `index.html`: the same information, in one short line, in the footer. Commit.

## Why this matters

A licence turns "please don't copy this" into "here is what you may do", and credits are how the people whose work you used get recognised. Employers who read your repository notice both: they show you understand that code is shared under rules.

## Done when

- [ ] A `LICENSE` file is on your repository's front page, with the year and your name.
- [ ] Every library, image, and font you did not make is credited, with its licence.
- [ ] Your README and your collection page's footer say which licence covers the code, and what covers the words and pictures.
