# Challenge 2: Creative

**Optional.** Roughly 45–60 minutes.

Make it personal: root your spatial layout in a place, tradition, or community you know.

## Task

1. In `spatial-layout.md`, design at least one part of your scene (a room, a display, a path) around a specific real-world reference from your own culture, city, or community — a market stall layout, a family home's courtyard, a local landmark's arrangement of space.
2. Note in the document what real reference inspired this choice and why it fits your capstone's audience.
3. Check that your scene graph (`scene-graph.md`) reflects this layout choice as an actual entity, not only a description.

## Why this matters

Spatial design is rarely neutral: real places carry meaning. Grounding your layout in something you know well usually produces a more legible, more memorable scene than an arbitrary arrangement of boxes.

## Done when

- [ ] At least one spatial choice is traced to a real personal or cultural reference.
- [ ] The scene graph includes an entity reflecting that choice.
- [ ] No claim is made that a real place or institution has endorsed this design.
