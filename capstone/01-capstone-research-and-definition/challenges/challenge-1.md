# Challenge 1: Foundation

**Required.** Roughly 45 minutes.

Write the "elevator pitch" your brief and stakeholder map cannot show on their own: why this project, why now.

## Task

1. In a new section at the end of `brief.md`, write a single paragraph (60–100 words) that answers: what is broken today, for whom, and why does it matter that someone fixes it now, rather than in a year?
2. Read it aloud. If it takes longer than 30 seconds to say, cut it down.
3. Ask one other person (a classmate, friend, or family member) to read it and tell you, in their own words, who it is for. If they get it wrong, revise it.

## Why this matters

A stakeholder map lists facts; a brief lists scope. Neither one, on its own, tells a busy mentor or partner why they should care. The elevator pitch is the sentence that earns you the rest of the conversation — and writing it forces you to know, precisely, what your project is for.

## Done when

- [ ] The pitch names a specific audience, not "everyone."
- [ ] It is under 100 words and reads naturally aloud.
- [ ] Someone outside your own head has confirmed they understood it correctly.
