# Challenge 3: Explorer

**Optional stretch.** Roughly 90 minutes.

Ground your brief in a real conversation, not only your own assumptions.

## Task

1. Identify one real person close to your intended audience: a friend, family member, or classmate who fits the profile in your problem statement (or who knows someone who does).
2. Ask them three or four short questions about the problem your capstone addresses: has it affected them, how do they deal with it today, what would make a solution genuinely useful to them.
3. Add a short "What I heard" section to `brief.md` with one direct, attributed quotation (first name only, with their permission) and two or three sentences on how it changed or confirmed your plan.
4. If anything they said moves an item between your "in scope" and "out of scope" lists, update those lists and note why.

## Why this matters

Every assumption in a brief is a risk until it meets a real person. A single short conversation, done honestly, often does more to sharpen scope than another hour of solo writing — and it is good practice for user research in Stage 2.

## Done when

- [ ] One real conversation happened, with someone's genuine consent to be quoted.
- [ ] The brief includes at least one direct, attributed quotation.
- [ ] Scope was reconsidered in light of what you heard, even if it did not change.
