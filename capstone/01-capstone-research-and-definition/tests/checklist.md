# Project check

Work through this list before you submit.

## Brief
- [ ] The problem statement names a specific audience and their problem.
- [ ] The scope lists at least three things you will build, and at least three you will not.
- [ ] At least one risk and one open question are written down honestly.
- [ ] The brief notes anything you already know about your audience's accessibility needs.

## Stakeholder map
- [ ] Every stakeholder who is affected by, or can affect, the project is listed.
- [ ] Each stakeholder's stake and the decisions they can make are recorded.
- [ ] A communication plan (how and how often) exists for each stakeholder.

## Mentor approval
- [ ] The brief and stakeholder map were sent to a mentor, instructor, or trusted reviewer.
- [ ] `mentor-approval.md` records a decision: Approved, Approved with changes, or Not yet approved.
- [ ] Any requested changes have been made and re-recorded.

## Quality
- [ ] All three documents use plain, specific language, with no invented facts.
- [ ] Every heading in `completed/index.html` (or your own copy) follows a logical order.
- [ ] The browser console has no errors when `starter/index.html` or `completed/index.html` is opened.

## 3D and XR (manual)

Automated tools cannot see inside a 3D canvas, so these are checked by a person. See [`docs/en/xr-accessibility.md`](../../../docs/en/xr-accessibility.md).

- [ ] If your capstone will include a 3D or XR prototype later, your brief already notes who your audience is in accessibility terms (screen reader use, connection speed, reading language).
- [ ] Nothing in this stage's pages autoplays motion, sound, or video.
