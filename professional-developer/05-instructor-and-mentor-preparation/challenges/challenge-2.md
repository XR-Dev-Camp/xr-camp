# Challenge 2: Creative

**Optional.** Roughly 45-60 minutes.

Root your lesson topic, your examples, or your mentor plan in your own language, culture, or community.

## Task

1. If you wrote a lesson: rewrite one worked example, one Common mistake, or your scene's shapes and colours so they reference something from your own culture or community — a pattern, a game, a landmark, a local craft — instead of a generic placeholder. Keep every fact accurate; if you are not certain of a detail, phrase it cautiously or leave it out, exactly as this course has taught you to do for the Women to Know spotlights.
2. If you wrote a mentor plan: add a short section on how you would adapt your mentoring style for someone learning in their second or third language, or for a community where asking a "beginner question" out loud is discouraged. Name one concrete thing you would say or do differently, not just an intention.
3. Explain your choice in one or two sentences at the top of the change, so a reviewer understands why it is there.

## Why this matters

XR Camp is a free school for women in Latin America and China. A lesson full of examples from only one culture quietly tells everyone else that this space was not built with them in mind. Small, specific, accurate choices — a real local reference instead of a generic one — do more for belonging than any general statement about inclusion.

## Done when

- [ ] At least one concrete part of your lesson or plan reflects your own language, culture, or community.
- [ ] Every fact you added is accurate, and anything uncertain is phrased cautiously or left out.
- [ ] A short note explains why the change is there.
