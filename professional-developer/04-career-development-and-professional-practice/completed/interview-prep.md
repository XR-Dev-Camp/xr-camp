# Interview preparation

## Portfolio walkthrough script

A walkthrough should run about three to five minutes without being interrupted, but every step below has a natural pause point if someone asks a question.

1. **Open with the problem, not the tool.** "This is a virtual cultural exhibit I built so it works whether or not you have a headset." Not: "This uses three.js and A-Frame." The tool comes later, once the interviewer knows why it matters.
2. **Show the 2D fallback first.** Load the exhibit without touching the 3D view. This proves the accessibility claim instead of just stating it.
3. **Show one interaction by keyboard only.** Tab to an exhibit, select it with Enter, and read its scene description aloud. Say what WCAG problem this solves in one sentence: "A visitor using a screen reader gets the same information a sighted visitor gets by looking around."
4. **Show the WebXR mode briefly, if a headset or the Immersive Web Emulator is available.** If not, say so plainly and show a screenshot instead; do not pretend to demonstrate something you cannot show live.
5. **Name one real trade-off.** For example: "I chose to lazy-load exhibits the visitor has not reached yet, which meant writing a small loading state instead of loading everything up front." A trade-off shows judgement; a list of features does not.
6. **Close with what you would do next.** One sentence on what you would add or fix with more time. This signals that you know the project is not perfect, which is more credible than claiming it is finished.

## Questions to ask the interviewer

Asking questions is not a formality at the end; it is how you find out whether the work fits you.

- "What does a typical week look like for this role, and how much of it is meetings versus focused work?"
- "How does the team communicate across time zones, if it is distributed?" (Ask this directly if the role is remote; do not assume everyone works your hours.)
- "What tools or frameworks does the team use day to day, and is there budget for training if I need to learn one?"
- "What would success look like in the first three months?"
- "How does the team give feedback on someone's early work?"
- "Is there anything about my background or portfolio that makes you hesitate? I would rather address it now."

That last question is optional and can feel bold; use it only if the conversation has been going well and you want direct, useful feedback.

## Before the interview

- Test your internet connection and camera or microphone at least an hour ahead, not five minutes ahead.
- If the interview crosses time zones, write down the meeting time in your own local time as well as the time zone the interviewer gave you, and double check which one a calendar invite actually shows.
- Have your CV (`index.html`), your case study, and one thing you are proud of open in separate tabs, ready to share your screen.
