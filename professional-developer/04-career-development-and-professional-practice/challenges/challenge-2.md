# Challenge 2: Creative

**Optional.** Roughly 45-60 minutes.

Root your case study and proposal in a project or problem from your own language, culture, or community, instead of a generic example.

## Task

1. In `freelancing-basics.md`'s proposal template, replace the imagined project with a real, specific need you have noticed in your own community: a local organisation without an accessible website, a small business that could use a simple online presence, or a cultural group that could use a small 3D or web exhibit like the one you built in Phases 3-4.
2. Rewrite the proposal's "What I understand you need" and "What I will deliver" sections around that real need.
3. Add one or two sentences to your case study explaining what, if anything, about your virtual cultural exhibit connects to your own culture or community (a story, an object, a tradition you chose to represent, or one you would add if you rebuilt it).
4. If you are comfortable, write your CV's role line or your LinkedIn-style headline in a way that reflects your own voice, rather than a generic industry phrase translated from someone else's.

## Why this matters

The clients most visible to a global platform are not the same ones most visible in your own city or region. Naming a real, local need, and being willing to describe your own culture in your professional writing rather than erasing it, often makes your work more findable, not less.

## Done when

- [ ] The proposal describes a real, specific need from your own context.
- [ ] The case study names a genuine connection to your own culture or community, or honestly says there is not one yet.
- [ ] Your own voice is visible somewhere in the package, not only a generic industry phrase.
