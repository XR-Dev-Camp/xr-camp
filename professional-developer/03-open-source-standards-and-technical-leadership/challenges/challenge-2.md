# Challenge 2: Creative

**Optional.** Roughly 45-60 minutes.

Root your explainer and your mini-project in your own language, culture, or community, instead of a generic example.

## Task

1. Pick a real accessibility, language, or connectivity problem you have noticed for beginners in your own community (for example: a font that drops accents in a script you use, an interface term that translates badly, or a tool that assumes reliable, unmetered internet).
2. Rewrite your explainer's Introduction and Goals sections around that problem instead of the scene-description example.
3. Rename your mini-project so its purpose reflects that problem, and update `GOVERNANCE.md`'s "What this project does" line to match.
4. Add one sentence to `standards-plan.md`, section 2, naming which W3C Community Group's work already touches this problem, even loosely (for example, an internationalization or accessibility group).

## Why this matters

Standards and open-source projects are more useful, and more likely to be adopted, when they solve a problem someone actually has. The problems most visible to Silicon Valley are not the same ones most visible in Latin America or China; naming your own is a real contribution to the conversation, not only a class exercise.

## Done when

- [ ] The explainer's Introduction and Goals describe a real, specific problem from your own context.
- [ ] The mini-project's name and description match the new problem.
- [ ] A Community Group connection is named, even loosely.
