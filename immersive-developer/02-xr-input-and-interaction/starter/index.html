The exhibit becomes an interaction lab

The same three objects from 4.1: a clay pot, a woven basket ring, and a jade stone. This lesson adds ways to reach into the scene: a floating in-world menu you point at, a jade stone you can pick up directly, tracked-hand support where a device offers it, and a button to place a virtual jade stone onto a real surface with AR. Every one of these has a full alternative below that needs no headset at all.

Everything in the lab

This list is not only a fallback: it is the same information as the picture, in words, so it is always here (WCAG 1.3.1).

    1. Look around

    Drag the view with a mouse or a finger. Or focus the lab (press Tab) and use the arrow keys. These two buttons do the same as dragging left or right, without a mouse.

    2. Animation

    The jade stone turns slowly on its own. Nothing else moves by itself, and the camera never moves unless you move it, on a screen, in a headset, or in AR.

    3. Enter VR: point, press, grab

    In VR, a small floating menu offers the same three actions as the buttons on this page: Pause, Rebuild, and Reset view. Point a controller or a tracked hand at a button and press the trigger, or pinch, to use it. Hold the grip button (or pinch near the jade stone with a hand) to pick it up directly, then release to put it back.

    4. Enter AR: place an object in your room

    If your browser and device support AR with surface detection ("hit-test"), this button lets you place a virtual jade stone onto a real, flat surface such as a table or floor, and see it stay there as you move around it.

    Checking whether this browser and device support AR placement…

    5. Direct grab (2D alternative)

    The keyboard-and-mouse equivalent of holding the grip button near the jade stone in VR: it lifts into your hand, then returns to its pedestal.

    6. Placement (2D alternative)

    The keyboard-and-mouse equivalent of AR hit-test placement: places a second jade stone a short distance in front of the camera's starting position, the same idea as placing one on a real table.
